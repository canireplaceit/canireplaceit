in the shipped emails

The blocks themselves are plain text-and-border HTML, so they embed in email as-is (tables + inline styles). The device minimaps ship as small generated PNGs — the repo's OG-image pipeline already renders per-page cards, so the same script renders each placement's three-width minimap once per slot, not per email.

constraints the shipped version must respect

Email is 2009 CSS. The design above is built for that.

Everything shown survives translation to email-safe HTML. What that translation means, concretely:

layout
One 600px column, table-based

The card becomes a centered <table> with inline styles on every cell. No flexbox, no grid, no <style> reliance — Gmail clips >102KB and Outlook desktop renders with Word.

fonts
System stacks only

No webfonts: system-ui, Segoe UI, Arial for text, ui-monospace, Menlo, Consolas for figures and links. The wordmark is TEXT, not an image — it renders everywhere, in every mode, before images load.

logo
The mark ships as a hosted PNG @2x with alt text

Gmail strips inline SVG. A 52×52 PNG of the two-arrow mark (light + dark variant via the dark-mode block), alt="canireplaceit", and the text wordmark beside it carries the identity when images are blocked — which is the default in Outlook and most corporate mail.

button
Bulletproof: a padded <a> with background-color

Border-radius degrades to square in Outlook — fine. The raw URL always follows in the dashed box, because an HTML-only link is invisible in a text client and scores as spam (the current code already knows this).

stamp
transform: rotate(-4deg), degrading to straight

Gmail and Apple Mail render the tilt; Outlook desktop ignores transform and shows a straight bordered badge — still legible, still green or red. The slam animation is web-only and does not ship in email.

dark mode
One @media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) block

Honored by Apple Mail and iOS; Gmail instead auto-inverts. The palette is chosen to survive both: no pure-black text, no color-meaningful backgrounds, buttons that stay legible inverted. The ☾ toggle above previews the block.

text part
text/plain mirrors every fact

Already policy in mail.ts. The redesigned text parts (toggle above) keep the receipt as aligned columns and the role card as ✓/✗ lines — the plain part is designed, not generated.

honesty
No tracking pixels — and the footer says so

The site's whole brand is honest counting. The emails carry it: no open-tracking, a "why you got this" line instead of an unsubscribe link nobody needs on transactional mail, and "replies reach a person" where that is true.

what changes, and why

Proposals

1 · An identity header on every email

Mark + text wordmark + a quiet right-aligned tag naming the email's kind (sign-in / receipt / campaign / team). Today the emails are anonymous mono paragraphs with a green that appears nowhere on the site.

2 · The receipt email is drawn as a receipt

Big mono total, dashed rows for placements / term / payment method / status, perforated edge — the same receipt language as the product page's price block. It doubles as the invoice.

3 · Verdict stamps carry the campaign verdicts

LIVE in green, NOT APPROVED in red — the site's signature element, reused where an email is literally delivering a verdict. Degrades to a straight badge in Outlook.

4 · Subjects state the fact, preheaders state the consequence

"Receipt: $486 — your ad is in review" beats "Your canireplaceit ad — payment received, in review". Every email gets a hand-written preheader — the second line of the inbox row is free advertising for tone.

5 · The invite spells out the permission boundary

A ✓/✗ role card replaces the prose sentence. "You cannot buy, change or take down anything" is the sentence that makes an invite safe to click — it deserves typography.

6 · Refunds lead the rejection

The subject already says "$486 refunded". Bad news emails are judged on the first five words; the money coming back is the first thing a rejected sponsor wants to know.

7 · Sponsor emails show the bought thing, in place

The receipt and the live email embed the actual ad blocks rendered from the sponsor's creative, plus a minimap of each page with the position highlighted (see the Ad formats tab). A sponsor should never have to visit the site to know what they bought looks like. Blocks embed as plain HTML; minimaps ship as PNGs from the existing OG-image pipeline.

8 · The footer becomes a trust block

Why you got this · no tracking pixels · replies reach a person. Three lines, same on every email, all true.

9 · Implementation shape

One emailShell(kind, bodyHtml) helper in mail.ts wrapping the shared header/footer/palette, five body builders, text parts hand-written next to their HTML. No template engine, no MJML dependency — the emails are five functions today and should stay five functions.
